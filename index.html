    How this is calculated

    Quantity = amount ÷ price. If a dosa cost about ₹6, then ₹500 ÷ ₹6 ≈ 83 dosas. Countable things (dosas, tickets) are rounded down to whole items.

    Gold is priced per 10 grams, so grams = amount ÷ price per 10 g × 10.

    “Worth in today’s money” (the chart below) = amount × price index today ÷ price index then. It uses a simple consumer-price index stored with the rest of the data.

    Every price comes from one editable file, js/data.js. The numbers are rounded approximations meant to illustrate the idea, not official statistics.

        Inflation, simply

        Why does ₹500 feel smaller?

        1. Prices generally rise over time. That slow, steady rise is called inflation.
        2. Your note stays exactly the same. A ₹500 note is still worth ₹500. Nothing about the paper changes.
        3. So the same note buys fewer things. Each rupee buys a little less than it used to. That’s “purchasing power” going down.

        Data transparency

        Where do these numbers come from?

        This is sample data. The prices below are rounded, approximate figures chosen to demonstrate the idea. They have not been verified against official records and should not be quoted as historical fact.

        Why any historical price is only approximate

        • City: a dosa in Chennai and one in Delhi never cost the same.
        • Month and year: fuel and gold prices change many times a year.
        • Product quality: a roadside dosa and a restaurant dosa are different products.
        • Market price vs. official price: subsidies, taxes and discounts all move the number.
        • Data source: different surveys and indices measure prices in different ways.
